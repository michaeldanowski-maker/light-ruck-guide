Cheap and DIY rucking weight: what to use

You don't need a plate to start. Here's what works, and how to keep it safe.

  • Water bottles or jugs. A US gallon of water weighs about 8.3 lb, so a couple of filled bottles is an easy first load. Fill them to the top so they don't slosh, cap them tight, and put them in a plastic bag in case of leaks. You can pour some out if the walk feels too hard.
  • Homemade sandbags. For "how to make sandbags for rucking," the simple method: fill a heavy-duty freezer bag or contractor bag with sand, squeeze out the air, seal it, then put it inside a second bag and wrap it with duct tape. Weigh each one on a bathroom scale and write the weight on it. Several small bags are easier to pack and adjust than one big one. Store-bought sand is cleaner than dirt or gravel.
  • Books or bags of rice. Fine for short test walks. Wrap books so corners don't dig in, and keep them flat against your back.
  • Dumbbells or kettlebells. They work in a pinch, but hard, round shapes can roll around. Wrap them in a towel and pack them so they can't move.

Packing tips for any weight: put it high in the pack, close to your back, and fill gaps with clothes or a towel so it can't shift. Make sure nothing hard presses into your spine. Don't load past your pack's listed maximum. Start light on flat ground, and stop if you feel pain in your back, knees, neck or shoulders.

How to choose a ruck plate

  1. Measure your pack first. If it has a plate sleeve, check the size (usually 10x12) and buy a plate listed for it. If you're using a regular backpack, any compact plate that fits the laptop sleeve will do.
  2. Start with 10 lb or less. It's cheaper, and you can add a second plate or a water bottle later instead of buying a heavier plate you won't use yet.
  3. Compare cost per pound, not just price. Cast iron is cheapest per pound; water and flexible plates cost more but adjust easily.
  4. Pad anything that isn't an exact fit. A towel or foam block keeps a smaller plate from sliding to the bottom of the pack.

Beginner safety note: This page is general information, not medical advice. Begin with a light load and short walks, and add weight slowly once walks feel easy. If you have a heart, joint, back, bone-density or pregnancy-related condition, check with a doctor or physical therapist first.
