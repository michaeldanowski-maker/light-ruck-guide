Rucking is just walking with weight on your back. You don't need a $300 pack or a gym. You need a backpack you already own, a little weight, and a schedule that builds up slowly. This guide covers how to start rucking: how much weight to carry, a simple 4-week rucking schedule for beginners, and the cheapest gear that makes it easier, with last-seen prices. The plan follows guidance from Cleveland Clinic, GORUCK and WebMD (sources below each section). It's general information, not medical advice.

Quick answer

  • Start with what you have. Any sturdy backpack with padded straps works. Cleveland Clinic's sports medicine doctor suggests starting with an empty pack or about 5 lb, and GORUCK says "any backpack works."
  • Ruck 2 times a week for about 20 minutes, walk normally on other days, and take at least one rest day.
  • Change one thing per week: time, distance or weight, not all three. Cleveland Clinic suggests about 10% a week; GORUCK suggests no more than 5 lb a week.
  • Cheapest upgrade: a Philosophy Gym 10 lb cast iron ruck weight (last seen $20.99) in your own backpack. For tiny weight steps, the ModGear Water Weight ($29.00) lets you add water a little at a time.

Key factors before your first ruck

  • Can you already walk 30 minutes comfortably? WebMD suggests being able to walk at least 30 minutes without feeling tired or short of breath before adding a rucksack. If not, build up your unweighted walking first.
  • Rucking weight for beginners: start light. Sources differ. Cleveland Clinic suggests about 5 lb (or an empty pack) to start; WebMD says 5–10 lb; GORUCK suggests 10–20 lb ("when in doubt" lighter) for people who already exercise. If you're new to exercise or over 50, starting at the low end is the cautious choice.
  • Keep the weight high and tight. Weight that sits high on your upper back and doesn't shift is more comfortable. Wrap loose weights (dumbbells, books, water bottles) in a towel so they don't poke or slide.
  • Shoes and socks. Supportive walking shoes you already walk in are fine for a light ruck. Breathable clothes help, and a shirt under the straps cuts down on chafing.
  • Pace and posture over load. Stand tall with your shoulders back. GORUCK's beginner pace benchmark is about 17–20 minutes per mile; if you can't keep good posture, the load is too heavy.

A 4-week rucking schedule for beginners

This is a simple, conservative plan built from the guidance above. Two rucks a week, with normal walking on other days and at least one full rest day. Adjust it to how you feel; repeating a week is fine.

Week Rucks per week Time per ruck Weight What changes
Before you start 0 — — Walk 30 minutes comfortably without a pack
Week 1 2 20 min Empty pack or about 5 lb Learn how the pack sits
Week 2 2 22 min Same as week 1 A little more time
Week 3 2 22 min Add up to 5 lb (about 10 lb total) A little more weight, same time
Week 4 2 25 min Same as week 3 A little more time

After week 4: keep alternating small increases in time or weight. GORUCK suggests adding no more than 5 lb a week and no more than 10% to weekly distance, and not exceeding about 1/3 of your bodyweight in the long run. Most people walking for fitness never need to go near that.

Prefer distance to minutes? At a 17–20 minute-per-mile pace, 20 minutes is about one mile. GORUCK suggests starting with 1–2 miles, 1–2 times a week.

Signs to back off: pain (not just normal muscle soreness) in your back, hips, knees, feet, neck or shoulders; leaning forward to carry the load; or a pace that drops well below your normal walk. Take an extra rest day and go lighter next time. Cleveland Clinic warns that starting too heavy or too far can lead to injuries like stress fractures.

For context, the CDC recommends adults get at least 150 minutes of moderate activity a week plus 2 days of muscle-strengthening activity. Two short rucks plus your regular walks can count toward the activity part.

How to choose your first rucking setup

  1. Do weeks 1–2 with what you own. An empty backpack or one with a few water bottles (about 2.2 lb per liter) tells you whether you like rucking before you spend anything.
  2. Buy weight before a pack. If your backpack is comfortable, a $21–$24 cast iron weight or the $29 water weight is all you need for the first month or two. More plate and DIY options are in our budget ruck plates guide.
  3. Upgrade the pack when weight starts to shift or straps dig in. That's when a plate-sleeve pack like the Polyfit or Wolf Tactical earns its price. Compare more packs in our budget rucking backpacks guide.
  4. Check plate fit. Plate packs are built for 10x12 plates. Smaller cast iron weights (like the Philosophy Gym and Yes4All 10 lb) can slide around in a plate sleeve, so pad them or buy a 10x12 plate.
  5. Vest or pack? Both work for a weighted walk. If you'd rather wear a vest, see our budget weighted vests for walking.

FAQ

How do I start rucking as a complete beginner? Make sure you can walk about 30 minutes comfortably, then put on a backpack with little or no weight and walk 20 minutes, twice a week. Add a little time or a little weight each week, one at a time.

How much weight should a beginner use for rucking? Start light. Cleveland Clinic suggests about 5 lb or even an empty pack; WebMD suggests 5–10 lb. GORUCK's range of 10–20 lb is aimed at people who already work out. When in doubt, go lighter and walk a bit longer instead.

How often should I ruck as a beginner? One or two times a week to start, per GORUCK and WebMD. Walk without weight on other days and take at least one rest day a week. GORUCK doesn't recommend rucking every day.

How far should I ruck as a beginner? About 1–2 miles, or 15–20 minutes, is a common starting point (GORUCK, WebMD). At a beginner pace of 17–20 minutes per mile, a 20-minute ruck is about one mile.

What can I use as weight for rucking? Water bottles, books, canned goods, a wrapped dumbbell or a homemade sandbag all work, per Cleveland Clinic and GORUCK. Weigh them and wrap them in a towel so they don't shift. Our ruck plates guide covers cheap DIY options.

Do I need a special rucking backpack? No. Any sturdy backpack with padded straps works to start. A plate-sleeve pack holds weight higher and steadier, which matters more as the weight goes up.

How fast should I ruck? At a comfortable pace with upright posture. GORUCK gives 17–20 minutes per mile as a beginner benchmark; if you're much slower than 20 minutes per mile, WebMD and GORUCK both suggest lightening the load.
